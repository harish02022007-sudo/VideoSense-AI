ABSTRACT 
 
The rapid growth of Indian railway passengers has created a significant need for 
intelligent travel assistance systems. Millions of people travel long distances on 
trains every day, and a common problem faced by travellers is missing their 
destination station — either due to unfamiliarity with the route or because they fall 
asleep during a long journey. To address these critical challenges, the present project 
introduces an AI-Based Travel Agent, specifically a Train Sleep Alert System, that 
uses mobile sensors, GPS location tracking, and intelligent sleep detection to ensure 
passengers never miss their stop.The system incorporates multiple core modules: 
real-time train route visualization on a map, intermediate station tracking, sleep 
detection using screen inactivity analysis, a configurable alarm system with beep 
and voice alerts, and live GPS-based journey tracking. After the passenger sets their 
source and destination station, the app begins tracking their journey and displays the 
correct train route with all intermediate stations on an interactive map. As the train 
approaches the destination station within the configured alert threshold, the system 
first sends a soft notification. If the passenger does not respond — determined by 
detecting extended phone screen inactivity suggesting sleep — the system escalates 
to a loud beep alarm followed by a multilingual voice message informing the 
passenger of the approaching station and estimated arrival time. 
The application is built using React 19 with TypeScript for the frontend, MapLibre 
GL for map visualization, an Express.js backend with SQLite for railway data, and 
integrates the Web Speech API, Web Audio API, and Geolocation API for core 
functionality. The system supports multiple Indian languages for voice alerts and 
allows passengers to customize alarm timing, beep duration, sound type, and voice 
language. Together, these modules create a comprehensive solution that enhances 
passenger safety, reduces travel anxiety, and makes train journeys more comfortable 
and reliable across India.

CHAPTER 1 
INTRODUCTION 
In the modern era, Indian Railways serves as the backbone of transportation for 
millions of citizens across the country. With over 8 billion passenger journeys 
recorded annually, trains remain the most popular and affordable means of long -
distance travel. However, despite the comfort and convenience that train travel 
offers, passengers frequently face a critical problem: missing their destination 
station. This issue is especially common during night journeys or long-distance trips 
where passengers fall asleep, unaware that their stop is approaching. 
 
Traditional solutions such as setting a mobile phone alarm fail to account for 
dynamic factors like train delays, real -time position, or the passenger's actual 
distance from the destination. A fixed time-based alarm does not adjust when a train 
runs late or early, often leading to premature or missed alerts. Furthermore, if a 
passenger is deeply asleep, a standard notification may not be sufficient to wake 
them up. 
 
To address this critical need, the AI -Based Travel Agent — Train Sleep Alert 
System — is developed to intelligently monitor a passenger's journey in real -time. 
The system leverages GPS -based location tracking, train route data, and screen 
inactivity monitoring to determine when a passenger is asleep and to deliver a 
timely, escalating alert before the destination station arrives.

A unique aspect of this system is the integration of a two -stage alarm mechanism. 
The first stage sends a soft notification when the train approaches within the

Web Audio API combined with a multilingual voice message through the Web 
Speech API. The voice message personalizes the alert by  addressing the passenger 
by name and announcing the destination station along with the estimated arrival 
time. 
 
The system provides an interactive map view powered by MapLibre GL, showing 
the full train route from the source station to the destination, with all intermediate 
stations highlighted. Major junction stations are differentiated visually, and the 
train's current simulated or GPS -tracked position is displayed on the map in real -
time. Passengers can also view the remaining distance to their destination, the 
estimated time of arrival, and any delays compared to the scheduled timetable. 
 
Additional features include customizable alarm settings — passengers can set how 
early before arrival they want to be alerted, choose the beep sound type (Train Horn, 
Alarm Bell, or Emergency Siren), configure beep duration, select the voice alert 
language (English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, or Malayalam), and enter their name for a 
personalized wake-up message. The system also supports both a simulation mode 
for demonstration and a live GPS tracking mode for real journeys on mobile devices. 
 
Overall, the AI -Based Travel Agent transforms the train travel experience by 
combining real-time location intelligence, sleep detection, and a configurable multi-
stage alert system to ensure passengers always reach their destination safely and on 
time.

2.1 GPS AND LOCATION-BASED TRACKING SYSTEMS 
 
Global Positioning System (GPS) technology has become the cornerstone of modern 
navigation and tracking applications. Early location -based services relied on cell 
tower triangulation, which provided limited accuracy for dense urban or rural 
environments. According to Hightower & Borriello (2011), the integration of  GPS 
receivers in mobile devices enabled precise real -time positioning with accuracy 
within 3-5 meters, making it suitable for vehicle and passenger tracking in transport 
systems. 
In the context of railway travel, GPS-based tracking systems have been deployed by 
major railway networks to monitor train positions, manage scheduling, and provide 
passenger information systems. Studies by Kumar & Singh (2018) highlight that 
real-time GPS integration with mapping APIs enables dynamic route visualization 
 
 
and live position updates. The Geolocation API provided by modern web browsers 
allows web applications to access GPS coordinates natively without requiring a 
dedicated mobile application, which significantly simplifies deployment across 
devices. 
 
Research by Chen et al. (2020) demonstrates that combining GPS data with pre -
loaded route geometry enables accurate identification of a passenger's current 
position along a known route, calculation of remaining distance to the destination, 
and estimation of arrival time based on average speed. These capabilities form the 
foundation of the journey tracking module in this project. The use of the Turf.js 
geospatial library further enhances spatial calculations, enabling precise distance 
computation between GPS coordinates and route waypoints.

Human activity and inactivity detection using mobile devices has been an active 
area of research across healthcare, productivity, and safety domains. Traditional 
sleep detection approaches relied on dedicated hardware such as wristband 
 
 
accelerometers or eye-tracking cameras. However, with the proliferation of 
smartphones, passive software -based methods have emerged as practical 
alternatives. Studies by Lane et al. (2014) demonstrate that mobile phone usage 
patterns, including screen on/off states and touch interaction frequency, serve as 
reliable proxies for estimating user alertness and sleep onset. 
 
 
The Web Page Visibility API, standardized by the W3C, provides a browser -level 
event that fires when a device screen turns off or the application enters background 
mode. Research on digital wellbeing applications (Google, 2019) confirms that 
screen time tracking using this API yields accurate measurements of device 
inactivity periods. In the context of travel safety, monitoring continuous screen 
inactivity for a threshold period (typically 30 -60 minutes) provides a reasonable 
signal for sleep detection without requiring invasive hardware. 
 
Passive interaction tracking through touch events, mouse movements, and keyboard 
input complements screen state monitoring. By recording the timestamp of the last 
user interaction and comparing it against the current time, the system can calculate 
the duration of inactivity. This approach, described by Cao et al. (2021) as 
lightweight behavioral monitoring, achieves reliable alertness estimation with 
minimal computational overhead — a critical requirement for mobile deployment 
on battery-constrained devices.

The purpose of system analysis is to understand the existing challenges faced by 
Indian railway passengers and to define the functional, non-functional, and technical 
requirements for building an effective AI -powered travel assistance solution. This 
chapter examines the limitations of current systems, identifies the scope of 
improvement, and outlines the major requirements of the proposed system: an AI -
Based Travel Agent with real-time route tracking, sleep detection, and a multi-stage

3.1 PROBLEM DEFINITION 
 
Millions of passengers travel by Indian Railways every day, covering distances 
ranging from a few hundred to several thousand kilometres. During these journeys, 
especially overnight or long -distance trips, passengers frequently encounter the 
following problems: 
 
■ Passengers unfamiliar with a route cannot identify their destination station 
visually from outside the train, particularly in the dark or when stations have 
similar-looking infrastructure. 
 
■ Long journeys cause fatigue, and passengers who fall asleep risk missing their 
destination station entirely, sometimes travelling hundreds of kilometres past 
it before realizing.

Indian passengers. 
 
■ Passengers who are hearing -impaired or deep sleepers do not respond to

mechanisms.

4. To design a two -stage configurable alarm system: a soft initial notification 
followed by a loud beep alarm  with multilingual voice alert if the passenger 
does not respond. 
5. To provide accurate intermediate station display along the route, with major 
junction stations highlighted, and arrival time estimation based on distance 
and schedule.

3.3 EXISTING SYSTEM ANALYSIS 
 
Existing solutions for train arrival alerts rely primarily on static time-based mobile 
phone alarms or basic SMS notification services offered by certain Indian Railway 
apps. These systems have the following key limitations: 
 
■ No real-time GPS-based position tracking along the specific train route. 
■ Fixed-time alarms do not adjust for train delays or early arrivals. 
■ No sleep detection capability — if the passenger does not hear the alarm, no 
further escalation occurs. 
■ No interactive map showing the train route with intermediate stations. 
■ Limited or no multilingual voice alert support for diverse Indian passengers. 
■ Poor accessibility for passengers who are unfamiliar with digital navigation 
tools.

SYSTEM DESIGN 
System design is a critical stage in software development that transforms analyzed 
requirements into a structured technical blueprint. The AI -Based Travel Agent 
requires an architecture that supports real -time GPS tracking, accurate train route 
visualization, sleep detection, multi-stage alarm triggering, and a responsive mobile

4.1 DESIGN APPROACH 
 
The design follows a modular approach, ensuring that each component operates 
independently while integrating seamlessly to form a unified platform. The three 
major layers in the system are: 
 
1. Frontend Interface Layer — React 19 + TypeScript + MapLibre GL + 
Tailwind CSS 
2. Backend and Database Layer — Express.js + SQLite + curated route JSON 
3. Device API Integration Layer — Geolocation, Web Audio, Web Speech, Page

4.2 DATABASE STRUCTURE 
 
The SQLite database uses a relational model optimized for station lookups and route 
point retrieval. The three core tables are:

4.4 DATA FLOW DESCRIPTION 
 
The data flow through the system follows this sequence: The user selects source and 
destination stations through the frontend search interface. The frontend sends a route 
request to the Express.js backend API, which queries the SQLite database for route 
points ordered by sequence. The backend validates the route geometry and returns 
the ordered station list with coordinates and schedule times. The frontend renders 
the route on the MapLibre GL map and begins the journey tracking loop. 
 
During the journey, GPS coordinates (or simulated positions) are continuously 
compared against route points using turf.js to determine the nearest station and 
remaining distance to the destination. The Sleep Detection Module runs a 
background check every 30 seconds. When the distance threshold is crossed, the 
Alarm System triggers Stage 1, and if no user interaction is detected within 2 
minutes, escalates to Stage 2 with audio and voice alerts. All user interaction 
timestamps are stored in React refs for zero-latency inactivity calculations.

5.2 CORE ALGORITHM IMPLEMENTATION 
 
The sleep detection algorithm combines two passive signals. The Page Visibility 
API fires a 'visibilitychange' event whenever the device screen turns off or the app 
enters background mode, updating the last -interaction timestamp. Simultaneously, 
touch, click, keydown, and mousemove events continuously refresh this timestamp 
whenever the user interacts with the app. The inactivity duration is calculated as the 
difference between the current time and the last recorded interaction. If this duration 
exceeds the configured sleep threshold (default: 60 minutes), the system  classifies 
the passenger as asleep. 
 
The distance-based alarm trigger uses turf.js to compute the great -circle distance 
between the current GPS position and the destination station coordinates on every 
position update. When this distance falls below the alert threshold (default: 
corresponding to 30 minutes at average train speed), Stage 1 is triggered. The arrival 
time is estimated by dividing the remaining distance by the average train speed (55 
km/h for express trains) and adding this to the current time, then reconciling with 
the scheduled arrival time from the route_points table. 
 
The beep alarm is generated using the Web Audio API without requiring audio files. 
Oscillator nodes create different waveforms for Train Horn (220Hz + 330Hz 
harmonics), Alarm Bell (rapid 880Hz pulses), and Emergency Siren (frequency -
swept 400 -1200Hz). The voice alert uses window.speechSynthesis.speak() with 
language codes mapped to Indian language voices (en -IN, hi-IN, ta-IN, te-IN, ml-
IN), delivering a personalized message with the passenger name and estimated 
arrival time.

5.3 EXECUTION WORKFLOW 
 
 
The execution workflow from app launch to alarm delivery follows these steps: 
 
 
1. User opens the app and searches for source and destination stations from the 
station database. 
2. Frontend sends route request to backend; backend queries SQLite for ordered 
route points and returns the station sequence. 
3. Map renders the complete train route polyline with all intermediate stations 
and highlights major junctions. 
4. User configures alarm settings (alert time, beep type, voice language, 
passenger name) and presses 'Start Live Journey' or 'Simulate Journey'. 
5. GPS tracking or animation loop begins, updating the train position on the map 
and recalculating distance to destination on every update. 
6. Sleep Detection Module begins monitoring screen inactivity in a background 
interval. 
7. When distance threshold is crossed, Stage 1 soft alert is displayed and a gentle 
chime plays. 
8. If no user interaction is detected within 2 minutes, Stage 2 activates: beep 
alarm plays for the configured duration and voice message is spoken. 
9. Full-screen alarm overlay appears; user taps 'Dismiss' to stop the alarm.

7.2 LIMITATIONS OF THE SYSTEM 
 
 
While the system performs effectively and fulfills its defined requirements, certain 
limitations were identified during development and testing: 
 
■ Dataset Coverage: The curated route dataset currently covers approximately 
100 major Indian express trains. Passengers on less common or regional train 
services may receive less accurate route information from the Gemini AI 
fallback. 
 
■ Sleep Detection Accuracy: The screen inactivity -based approach cannot 
distinguish between a sleeping passenger and one who is simply not using 
their phone while awake. This may cause false positives in some scenarios. 
 
 
■ Internet Dependency: The system requires an internet connection for initial 
route data loading and Gemini AI fallback queries. Fully offline operation is 
not yet supported. 
 
■ iOS Limitations: Web Speech API and background Geolocation have known 
restrictions on iOS Safari, which may reduce functionality when the app runs 
in the background on Apple devices.

accuracy across all train routes.
